Why it exists

Most meeting tools record you, summarise you, and upload you. Notewise does the whole thing on your own hardware — and nothing leaves your machine unless you send it.

How it works

Audio in.
Understanding out.

  1. 01 Capture

    Press record. Audio from your microphone is written straight to a SQLite file beside your other notes. Nothing is streamed anywhere.

  2. 02 Transcribe

    Whisper runs locally, GPU-accelerated on Apple silicon. Speakers are separated as it goes, so it reads as a conversation.

  3. 03 Understand

    A model you choose — Ollama on your machine, or your own key — pulls out decisions and commitments, each linked to the moment it was said.

  4. 04 Ask

    Question any meeting or the whole workspace. Every answer cites its sources, and says so plainly when the answer is not there.

Where your data goes

Nowhere, unless you send it

Most tools in this category say “private” and mean “encrypted on our servers”. Here it is a property of the architecture: the parts that leave your machine are the ones you switch on.

LOCAL

Recording, transcription, search, notes

Whisper runs on your hardware. Your workspace is a SQLite file you can open, back up, or delete. Search — including semantic search — is computed locally.

LOCAL

Summaries and answers, with Ollama

Point Notewise at a model running on your machine and nothing leaves it. This is the default, and the app tells you which mode you are in.

YOUR CHOICE

Summaries with a hosted model

Bring your own Anthropic, OpenAI-compatible or Gemini key and that transcript goes to that provider. Keys live in your OS keychain and no endpoint will read one back to you.

YOUR CHOICE

Connectors

Write meetings to a Markdown vault (stays local) or POST them to a webhook you control (does not). Each connector says which it is before you enable it.

Recognising people by voice is off by default, and switching it off erases what was stored. It identifies people who never installed anything — so it is a decision you make, not one made for you.

Read it before you trust it

The engine and the apps are MIT licensed. Every claim on this page is checkable against the source — including the one about nothing being uploaded, which is enforced by a server that refuses to bind to anything but loopback.
